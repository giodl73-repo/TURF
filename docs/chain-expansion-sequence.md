# Chain Expansion Sequence

TURF should add new chains in an order that teaches different kinds of retail
geography rather than only adding bigger lists.

## Decision

After the Home Depot/Lowe's foundation, the next sequence is:

1. Auto parts.
2. QSR restaurants.
3. Target/Walmart.
4. Fast casual restaurants.
5. Casual dining restaurants.
6. Costco as a later contrast layer.

## Why Auto Parts First

Auto parts chains create a denser neighborhood and corridor network than
home-improvement big boxes. They should help TURF learn smaller practical
service territories before it moves to broad national retail anchors.

Initial comparison set:

- AutoZone
- O'Reilly Auto Parts
- NAPA Auto Parts
- Advance Auto Parts

Working hypotheses:

- Auto parts will fill in many places where Home Depot/Lowe's have no direct
  row.
- Different auto parts brands may reveal older-suburb, highway-corridor,
  industrial, small-city, and rural county-seat patterns.
- NAPA may need extra review because the brand network can include independent
  or affiliated stores.

## Why QSR Second

QSR should come after auto parts because it is the next dense daily-life layer.
McDonald's, Burger King, Wendy's, and Taco Bell should show local commercial
grids, highway strips, ferry-side gaps, small-town anchors, and repeated
co-location patterns at a finer grain than big-box retail.

Initial QSR comparison set:

- McDonald's
- Burger King
- Wendy's
- Taco Bell

Working hypotheses:

- McDonald's acts as the broad default national QSR grid.
- Burger King and Wendy's may expose thinner challenger coverage.
- Taco Bell may reveal different daypart, corridor, and co-location patterns.
- QSR should help distinguish everyday neighborhood territories from regional
  destination-store territories.

## Why Target/Walmart Third

Target/Walmart is the stronger strategic territory contrast, but it should come
after dense local-service and QSR categories. Walmart and Target are broad
public signals of different market choices:

- Walmart should expose small-city, exurban, and regional-access geography.
- Target should expose denser suburban and urban-affluent nodes.
- The comparison can answer a direct TURF question: which places are Target
  places, Walmart places, both, or neither?

Initial Target/Walmart review must separate major format differences where the
source exposes them, especially Supercenter, Neighborhood Market, pharmacy-only,
small-format Target, and non-store service points.

## Fast Casual Later

Fast casual chains are more selective than QSR and should help TURF identify
urban, college-town, suburban-affluent, and lifestyle-center nodes. They should
come after QSR because their selectivity is easier to interpret against a dense
baseline.

Initial fast casual comparison set:

- Chipotle
- Five Guys

## Casual Dining Later

Casual dining chains are destination-oriented restaurant anchors. They should
help TURF separate family dining corridors, mall/lifestyle nodes, tourist
centers, and higher-selectivity regional markets.

Initial casual dining comparison set:

- Olive Garden
- Cheesecake Factory

## Costco Later

Costco remains a high-value contrast, especially in Puget Sound because of its
regional origin and destination-store footprint. It should follow
Target/Walmart and restaurant layers because its lower store count creates
broader regional catchment questions rather than the local neighborhood grids
TURF needs to learn first.

## Next Pulse Shape

The next restaurant pulse should use Overture Places to extract a bounded
Washington QSR candidate layer for McDonald's, Burger King, Wendy's, and Taco
Bell, review packet-ready rows, and compare Seattle/Bremerton ferry geography
against home improvement and auto parts.
