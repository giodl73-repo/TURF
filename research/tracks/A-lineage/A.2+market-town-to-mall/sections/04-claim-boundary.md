# Source Policy and Claim Boundary

## Source Policy

Allowed evidence:

- repo-generated TURF reports and CSVs,
- reviewed Overture-derived retail-complex, grocery, pharmacy, and mass-retail
  layers already present in the repo,
- public historical sources cited in `references.bib`,
- A.1 source-boundary rules when postal/addressability evidence is referenced.

Blocked evidence and claims:

- no proprietary mall, landlord, tenant, mobility, sales, or lease data,
- no scraped official retailer locators unless terms allow reuse,
- no claims about shoppers, residents, protected traits, income, spending,
  credit, culture, or neighborhood quality,
- no claims that malls or retail complexes are official towns, municipal
  centers, or corporate territories.

## Claim Boundary

This module may claim:

- retail complexes, malls, grocery/pharmacy grids, and mass-retail anchors are
  observable commercial-footprint layers,
- some TURF fields perform market-center functions by concentrating repeated
  retail and service evidence,
- mall-led, edge-city, everyday-service, and legacy-center patterns can be
  compared as terrain functions,
- historical market towns are useful functional analogies when sourced and
  bounded.

This module may not claim:

- malls descend directly from medieval market towns,
- commercial anchors define culture, identity, wealth, or resident behavior,
- retail complexes are legal civic boundaries,
- store presence proves customer base, trade area, or drive-time catchment,
- private company territories are known unless published.

## Modeling Boundary

A.2 is not a catchment model. It may prepare hypotheses for future catchment,
drive-time, gravity, or route-access work, but the scaffold itself is
descriptive and evidence-bounded.
