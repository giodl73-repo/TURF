# Town / Port / Depot / Highway Spine Matrix

## Purpose

The A.2 work should not stay trapped in post-office logic or in a single
Domino's example. The broader question is how historical enclave functions map
onto current TURF evidence:

- town and market provisioning,
- port or landing transfer,
- depot and intermodal service,
- highway-node corridor service,
- mall and edge-city comparison shopping,
- current multi-layer service enclaves.

Machine-readable matrix:
`tables/town-port-depot-highway-spine-matrix.csv`.

## What The Matrix Adds

The matrix turns the central spine into a work plan. Each row connects:

- the historical function,
- the current enclave family or proxy,
- the evidence already in the repo,
- example fields,
- the current read,
- the next gate,
- the claim boundary.

This keeps A.2 broad enough to study malls, towns, QSR/pizza, highway-service
fields, ports, depots, and current service enclaves without pretending they are
all the same thing.

## Current Read

Town / market:

Everyday-service stacks are the strongest current analogue for market-town
provisioning. Lincoln Park / Lakeview, Decatur / Emory, and Southlake Town
Square show this at different urban and suburban scales. Kingston shows the
thin version: one four-brand QSR row plus Domino's-style pizza presence.

Port / landing:

The evidence is scaffold-ready. South Philadelphia Sports / Port, Long Beach /
Lakewood, Camp Creek, and ferry-side Kingston/Bainbridge all point toward
movement infrastructure as enclave terrain. The missing step is a dedicated
port/landing/depot module that aligns ferry terminal, port/event, airport-edge,
logistics, and transit-center evidence.

Depot / intermodal:

Urban civic rail cores such as Center City / Market East, University City /
30th Street, Loop / River North, and Camden Waterfront / Cooper already appear
in the family consolidation. The depot analogy is plausible, but it needs a
specific station/intermodal sidecar before it becomes proof rather than
vocabulary.

Highway node:

This is the strongest next branch after town provisioning. The car-trip stack
already separates QSR, auto-parts, and gas/convenience terrain. Arlington /
Grand Prairie is the clearest all-three dense grid, and the pizza sidecar now
adds multi-brand pizza/carryout presence to that same field. Northeast
Roosevelt, Cherry Hill / Moorestown, Las Colinas / Irving, and Legacy / Frisco
/ Plano extend the branch into older auto corridors and gas/highway grids.

Mall / edge city:

Mall-led regional fields and edge-city big-box fields remain core A.2 terrain.
The important refinement is that service sidecars modify those fields rather
than replacing the retail-complex evidence. North Point / Alpharetta is useful
because the pizza sidecar adds multi-brand provisioning signal to an edge-city
mall field.

Current enclave:

The endpoint is not a direct historical descent claim. It is an observable
current stack. Older enclave functions reappear as layered evidence:
addressability, provisioning, transfer, corridor service, comparison shopping,
civic presence, and boundary disagreement.

## Decision

Keep A.2 focused on town, mall, edge-city, and provisioning terrain. Use the
new matrix as the handoff to an A.3 port / landing / depot / corridor-node
scaffold.

Do not restart broad post-office expansion unless a later claim specifically
needs A.1b's denominator method.

## Claim Boundary

The spine is a disciplined analogy. It does not prove official boundaries,
service adequacy, customer behavior, trade areas, freight flows, route choice,
commuter catchments, resident identity, or direct historical continuity. Each
row is only as strong as its scoped TURF evidence packet.
