## Read

A.2 should treat modern market-center terrain as a stack of functions, not as
one retail format.

The current TURF evidence separates at least six market-center expressions:

- mall-led regional fields,
- edge-city big-box power fields,
- neighborhood everyday-service fields,
- town-center / affluent service fields,
- car-oriented corridor enclaves,
- port / event / industrial civic fields.

This confirms the broader Track A spine: the useful unit is not "mall" alone.
It is the current enclave function: provisioning, comparison shopping,
movement support, civic/industrial transfer, or neighborhood daily service.

## Market-Town Functions In Current Evidence

The strongest direct market-town analogy is **provisioning concentration**.
Grocery/pharmacy grids, QSR clusters, and everyday-service stacks show repeated
daily-trip terrain. These should be read as modern town-service evidence, not
as proof of official town boundaries or resident identity.

The second market-town analogy is **comparison-shopping concentration**.
Mall-led regional fields and retail complexes concentrate comparison shopping
and anchor-store evidence. They can behave like modern regional market centers,
but the claim remains commercial-footprint only.

The third analogy is **corridor exchange and movement support**. QSR, gas /
convenience, auto parts, truck-stop, and highway-service stacks are closer to
port, depot, or highway-node lineage than parish lineage. Domino's-style QSR
coverage questions belong here: they are evidence about current provisioning
and delivery/service terrain, not evidence about minimum postal parishes.

The fourth analogy is **legacy-center persistence**. Legacy malls and older
service grids can retain market-center structure even when the current anchor
function is weaker or mixed. TURF should preserve this as a terrain type
without making decline, quality, or neighborhood-value claims.

## Evidence Signals

The enclave family consolidation reports 43 fields, 42 detailed labels, and 10
consolidated enclave families. The most relevant A.2 families are:

- `mall_led_regional_field`,
- `edge_city_big_box_power_field`,
- `car_oriented_corridor_enclave`,
- `affluent_town_center_field`,
- `neighborhood_everyday_service`,
- `port_event_industrial_civic_field`.

The car-trip stack comparison adds a service-terrain layer across 30
field-aligned metro fields. It distinguishes daily-trip food saturation, auto
service, and gas / convenience corridor texture. That distinction is important
because QSR signal alone does not mean the same thing in a downtown daily-trip
field, a highway-service corridor, a mall field, or a port/coastal grid.

## Boundary

This first read does not claim direct historical descent from market towns. It
does not claim customer behavior, spending, income, culture, resident identity,
trade area, catchment, drive-time coverage, or official civic boundary. It only
maps historical market-center functions to current TURF commercial-footprint
evidence.
