# Central Spine: Historical Enclaves To Current Enclaves

## Thesis

Track A should follow one central question:

How do older settlement functions reappear as modern public-infrastructure and
commercial-service enclaves?

The point is not to prove direct descent from medieval parishes, market towns,
ports, rail depots, or highway towns. The point is to use those older functions
as disciplined analogies for modern TURF evidence:

- addressability,
- provisioning,
- exchange,
- movement,
- civic presence,
- commercial anchoring,
- boundary disagreement.

## Spine

The working lineage spine is:

1. **Parish / postal parish**: local legibility, addressability, and civic
   presence.
2. **Town / market town**: recurring provisioning, exchange, and everyday
   service concentration.
3. **Port / landing / depot**: transfer points where movement infrastructure
   concentrates service and identity.
4. **Rail / highway / interchange node**: corridor-era successor to the depot
   or port function.
5. **Mall / retail complex / big-box field**: modern comparison-shopping and
   regional-service concentration.
6. **Service quarter / professional services**: public-facing finance,
   paperwork, agency, and skilled-service terrain.
7. **Current service enclave**: the observable present-day stack of retail,
   food, finance, civic, transit, and other anchors.

Each module should ask how a historical function maps to a current observable
field, then state what the analogy cannot claim.

## Near-Term Priority

Stop expanding post offices as the main workstream. A.1b is now a method
proving ground, A.2 is the provisioning / market-center layer, A.3 is the
reviewed movement-node layer, and A.4 is the synthesized service-quarter layer.

The next gate is a current-enclave claim-packet design or selected-field atlas
that overlays A.1/A.1b, A.2, A.3, and A.4 evidence. It should start with a
small number of high-information fields and explicitly mark observed layers,
profile gaps, source gates, and out-of-scope layers.

## Claim Boundary

The spine is an organizing model. It does not prove direct historical descent,
resident identity, service adequacy, retail trade areas, catchments, or official
boundaries. Every empirical claim still has to be tied to a scoped TURF
evidence packet.
