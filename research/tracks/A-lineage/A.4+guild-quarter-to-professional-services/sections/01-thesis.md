# Thesis

Older guild quarters, legal/financial streets, and office-service districts
organized public-facing skilled services near markets, civic cores, ports,
depots, and commercial streets. A.4 treats that as a functional analogy for
modern TURF service-quarter terrain.

The modern observable proxy is not a guild and not a formal office district.
It is a stack of public-facing service footprints:

- banks and credit unions,
- insurance agencies,
- branded tax-prep offices,
- local accounting, legal, real-estate, and professional-service offices when
  reviewed later.

The question is whether these rows cluster with existing enclave terrain:
market towns, malls, highway corridors, depot fields, port/event fields, and
current service enclaves.

## Claim Boundary

A.4 does not infer customer income, wealth, credit quality, insurance risk,
business quality, professional status, protected traits, or individual behavior.
It uses public footprint evidence only.
