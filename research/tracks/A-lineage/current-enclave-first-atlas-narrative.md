# Current Enclave First Atlas

Track A asks how older settlement functions reappear as modern public
infrastructure and commercial-service terrain. The answer is not direct
descent. A mall is not a medieval market town, a ferry terminal is not a port
parish, and a bank cluster is not a guild quarter. The useful claim is more
bounded: present-day fields can be read as stacks of observable public
footprints.

This first atlas uses six selected packets to show how the stack works.

## The Frame

Each packet is read through three current layers:

- provisioning and market terrain,
- movement and corridor terrain,
- service-quarter terrain.

Postal legibility remains the baseline method from A.1/A.1b, but it is not
scored inside these six packets. That keeps the atlas centered on the full
spine rather than pulling the work back into post-office expansion.

## Long Beach / Lakewood

Long Beach / Lakewood is the clean full-stack example. It joins provisioning /
market-bridge evidence, packet-ready port/logistics movement evidence, and full
service-quarter texture.

Its role is straightforward: this is the case to use when we need to show the
current-enclave stack working with the fewest caveats. It can support a
layered public-footprint claim. It cannot support a port authority boundary,
freight-flow, catchment, resident identity, demand, or service-adequacy claim.

## Arlington / Grand Prairie

Arlington / Grand Prairie is the highway-service full-stack comparison. It has
multi-brand pizza/provisioning, dense car-trip context, lodging, bounded
distribution support, and full service-quarter texture.

Its caveat is important. The truck-stop layer remains weak. The packet should
be read as highway-service texture with bounded logistics support, not as
truck-stop coverage, freight volume, route choice, trip purpose, or demand.

## Cherry Hill / Moorestown

Cherry Hill / Moorestown is the partial corridor-service comparison. It has
highway-node context, Holman truck-service / corridor-support evidence, and
full service-quarter texture.

This packet is useful because it prevents a false binary. The field has
truck-service support, but not a complete truck-stop, lodging, or logistics
layer. That distinction is the point of the packet.

## Loop / River North

Loop / River North is the depot/intermodal and service-profile-gap case. It has
direct station/transit evidence after retry and a strong professional-service
core.

Its caveat is the bounded loan/mortgage profile gap. The loan/mortgage regex
pass returned no packet-ready rows, but that is not lender absence. The packet
can support a depot/intermodal and professional-service terrain claim, not a
commuter catchment, official district boundary, income, credit, demand, or
lender-absence claim.

## Kingston WA

Kingston WA is the ferry-side design-only case. It has thin provisioning via a
single Domino's row, ferry route context, and direct ferry-terminal retry
evidence.

This packet should stay design-only. A.4 is not scored. Kingston is useful
because it shows how a ferry-side place can be thinly provisioned without
being a service-absence case. It should not be promoted to a full
current-enclave stack by implication.

## Kitsap Mall / Silverdale

Kitsap Mall / Silverdale is the light-control case. It has mall/provisioning
context and light service-quarter texture, while direct movement evidence
remains partial.

Its service-quarter texture is real but limited: finance, tax prep, insurance,
and real-estate layers are observed; LAN and loan/mortgage profiles are checked
absent only for bounded profiles. That is not legal/accounting/notary absence,
lender absence, or service inadequacy.

## Atlas Result

The six packets establish a usable first typology:

- clean full stack,
- highway-service full stack with caveat,
- corridor-service partial movement,
- depot/intermodal plus service profile gap,
- ferry-side design-only contrast,
- light-control service-quarter contrast.

The atlas also sets a discipline rule: choose the claim first, then close only
the evidence that would change that claim. More categories, more states, or
more generic business rows do not automatically improve the work.

## Claim Boundary

This atlas supports observable current-enclave stack classification only. It
does not establish official boundaries, direct historical descent, lived
identity, demographics, resident identity, service adequacy, catchments,
demand, traffic models, spending, freight flow, truck counts, trip purpose,
transaction volume, professional quality, service absence, or individual
behavior.
