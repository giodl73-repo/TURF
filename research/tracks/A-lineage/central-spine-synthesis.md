# Central Spine Synthesis

Track A now has enough evidence to treat the central spine as a cross-module
classifier, not a list of isolated analogies.

The spine has six practical roles:

1. Parish / postal parish: legibility, addressability, and civic presence.
2. Minimum postal parish: a method proving ground for sparse-node boundaries,
   denominators, exclusions, and truth dossiers.
3. Town / market / mall: provisioning, exchange, QSR, grocery/pharmacy, mass
   retail, mall, and everyday-service terrain.
4. Port / depot / highway node: movement, transfer, terminal, corridor-service,
   lodging, truck-service, and bounded logistics terrain.
5. Service quarter: public-facing finance, paperwork, agency,
   legal/accounting/notary, real-estate, and loan/mortgage office texture.
6. Current enclave stack: the observable present-day overlay of those layers.

## What Changed

The broader context is now explicit:

- Post offices are no longer the main expansion path. A.1b remains useful as a
  method proving ground.
- Domino's-style coverage, QSR, gas/convenience, auto-parts, and similar
  evidence belong to the town/provisioning and highway-node layers.
- Ferry terminals, depots, stations, airport edges, lodging, truck-service, and
  logistics evidence belong to the movement-node layer.
- Banks, tax prep, insurance, legal/accounting/notary, real-estate, and
  loan/mortgage evidence belong to the service-quarter layer.
- Consulting and generic business-service rows remain deferred unless a
  specific claim needs them.

## Next Gate

The next useful artifact is a current-enclave claim-packet design or selected
field atlas. It should show, for each selected field:

- which spine layers are observed,
- which layers are profile gaps,
- which layers are source-gated,
- which layers are out of scope,
- what claim boundary applies.

That packet should start with a small number of high-information fields rather
than broad national or category expansion.

## Claim Boundary

The central spine is an organizing classifier. It does not establish direct
historical descent, official boundaries, resident identity, demographics,
service adequacy, catchments, trip purpose, spending, demand, transaction
volume, professional quality, or individual behavior.
