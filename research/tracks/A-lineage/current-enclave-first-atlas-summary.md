# Current Enclave First Atlas Summary

The first selected-field atlas turns Track A's central spine into six readable
current-enclave cases. The point is not to rank places. The point is to show
how older settlement functions can be translated into bounded present-day
evidence layers without claiming direct descent, official boundaries, service
adequacy, catchments, or identity.

## Reading The Atlas

Each packet reads a selected field through three current layers:

- provisioning / market terrain,
- movement / corridor terrain,
- service-quarter terrain.

Postal legibility is held as a baseline method, not scored inside these six
packets. That keeps the atlas from sliding back into post-office expansion.

## Packet Types

Long Beach / Lakewood is the clean full-stack case. It has provisioning /
market-bridge evidence, packet-ready port/logistics movement evidence, and full
service-quarter texture. It is the best first example of the current-enclave
stack working cleanly.

Arlington / Grand Prairie is the highway-service full-stack comparison. It has
multi-brand pizza/provisioning, dense car-trip context, lodging, bounded
distribution support, and full service-quarter texture. Its caveat is that the
truck-stop layer remains weak, so it should be read as highway-service texture,
not truck-stop coverage.

Cherry Hill / Moorestown is the partial corridor-service comparison. It has
highway-node context, Holman truck-service / corridor-support evidence, and
full service-quarter texture. Its caveat is that this is partial
truck-service support, not a complete truck-stop, lodging, or logistics layer.

Loop / River North is the depot/intermodal plus service-profile-gap case. It
has direct station/transit evidence after retry and a strong professional
service core. Its caveat is that the bounded loan/mortgage regex profile is
checked absent only; that is not a lender-absence claim.

Kingston WA is the ferry-side design-only case. It has thin provisioning via a
single Domino's row, ferry route context, and direct ferry-terminal retry
evidence. A.4 is not scored, so Kingston should not be promoted to a full
current-enclave stack by implication.

Kitsap Mall / Silverdale is the light-control case. It has mall/provisioning
context and light service-quarter texture, while movement remains partial and
LAN / loan-mortgage gaps are bounded-profile results only.

## What The Atlas Proves

The atlas proves that Track A can now classify current-enclave terrain by
packet role:

- clean full stack,
- highway-service full stack with caveat,
- corridor-service partial movement,
- depot/intermodal plus service profile gap,
- ferry-side design-only contrast,
- light-control service-quarter contrast.

It also proves that more breadth is not automatically better. The useful next
work is claim-driven: pick the claim surface first, then close only the evidence
that would change that claim.

## What Not To Do

Do not treat Kingston as a service-absence case.

Do not treat Kitsap Mall / Silverdale as a full professional-service stack.

Do not treat Arlington / Grand Prairie or Cherry Hill / Moorestown as freight
volume or truck-stop coverage claims.

Do not treat Loop / River North's loan/mortgage profile gap as lender absence.

Do not restart broad post-office, generic business-service, or national
category expansion from this atlas.

## Next Gate

Choose one claim surface and close only the evidence that would materially
change it. The cleanest choices are:

- publish a narrative atlas using these six packets as examples,
- close the Arlington / Cherry Hill truck-service comparison if truck-stop
  coverage matters,
- close the Loop / River North loan/mortgage profile gap if lender-edge texture
  matters,
- score A.4 for Kingston only if ferry-side full-stack comparison matters.

## Claim Boundary

The atlas supports observable current-enclave stack classification only. It
does not establish official boundaries, lived identity, demographics, resident
identity, service adequacy, catchments, demand, traffic models, spending,
freight flow, truck counts, trip purpose, transaction volume, professional
quality, service absence, or individual behavior.
