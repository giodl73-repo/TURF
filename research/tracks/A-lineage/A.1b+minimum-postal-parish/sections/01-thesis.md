# Thesis

Working thesis:

> Some modern places may be legible as minimum postal parishes: addressable
> localities whose public footprint is anchored by a post office and only a
> small everyday-service stack.

This is the sparse counterpart to A.1. A.1 showed postal/address infrastructure
as an addressability and civic-service layer across selected TURF fields. A.1b
asks what happens at the bottom of the stack, where a place may have a post
office, a QSR, a gas station, a dollar store, or a grocery/pharmacy node, but
not a larger mall, big-box, or multi-brand retail field.

## Research Questions

- What is the minimum public footprint needed for a place to remain visible in
  TURF?
- Which places are post-office-only in the current evidence stack?
- Which places are post-office plus one everyday national-chain layer?
- Are minimum postal parishes more common in rural towns, islands, ferry
  communities, unincorporated places, highway stops, mountain towns, or county
  seats?
- What should TURF call a place that is addressable and routable but not
  strongly commercial?
