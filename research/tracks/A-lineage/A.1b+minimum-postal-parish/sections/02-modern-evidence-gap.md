# Modern Evidence Gap

TURF cannot yet identify the smallest postal parishes nationally.

The current cross-metro type-discovery profile has 43 selected bounded fields.
Those fields are intentionally anchor-heavy: urban cores, mall fields,
edge-city fields, corridors, service villages, and selected civic/retail
terrain. They are useful for learning the typology, but they are not a census
of small postal places.

## What Current Data Can Support

Current TURF data can support a pilot definition:

- identify low-observed-layer fields,
- distinguish checked absence from source gates,
- compare postal signal with QSR, gas/convenience, grocery, pharmacy, and
  retail-complex evidence,
- preserve evidence gaps instead of calling them true absence.

## What Current Data Cannot Support

Current TURF data cannot yet support:

- the smallest postal parish in the United States,
- counts of all post-office-only places,
- national rural coverage,
- complete unincorporated-place coverage,
- claims about population, income, culture, identity, or local quality,
- customer behavior or service adequacy claims.

## Why This Gap Matters

The gap is analytically important. If A.1 is about postal/addressability as a
layer inside many different enclave families, A.1b is about the minimum viable
enclave: the place where postal infrastructure may be the main public evidence
that a locality exists in national addressable life.
