# Synthesis

## Module Claim

A.1 can now make one bounded claim:

> Postal/address infrastructure is a recurring public addressability layer in
> TURF's current retail field evidence. It works as a modern parish-style
> analogy only at the level of function: local legibility, service access,
> administrative routing, and civic presence.

The claim is intentionally narrow. It joins three evidence classes:

- TURF evidence: observed post-office signal in 28 of 41 current post-office
  summary fields, with checked absences and source gates preserved.
- USPS/Census evidence: USPS delivery/address infrastructure and Census ZCTA
  approximations are public but distinct addressability/statistical-context
  layers.
- Historical analogy evidence: older parishes, market towns, record
  institutions, and settlement forms demonstrate recurring local-legibility and
  service-center functions, not direct modern equivalents.

## Why It Matters for TURF

TURF started with the intuition that the post office is an "original TURF"
layer. A.1 refines that into a usable research rule:

Postal geography should be treated as an addressability and civic-service layer
inside the field stack, not as the field itself.

That distinction helps explain why postal signal can appear across very
different enclave families:

- urban civic and rail cores,
- mall-led regional fields,
- edge-city big-box fields,
- older auto corridors,
- office and river secondary cores,
- port, event, and industrial civic fields.

It also explains why checked absence and source gates matter. A field without
observed post-office rows can still be a meaningful TURF field if the grocery,
pharmacy, mass-retail, retail-complex, finance, QSR, auto-parts, or
gas/convenience layers support it.

## Lineage Interpretation

The useful lineage is functional:

| Historical Function | Modern A.1 Translation | TURF Use |
|---|---|---|
| Local legibility | address, ZIP, delivery locality, ZCTA approximation | attach public geography context without claiming corporate territory |
| Civic/service point | post office and related public-facing infrastructure | identify civic addressability signal in bounded fields |
| Record and routing layer | administrative address systems and statistical joins | keep source postal, ZCTA, county, CBSA, and field labels separate |
| Market/service center | nearby retail and civic facilities | compare postal signal with everyday-service and retail-footprint layers |

This lineage does not say that postal geography caused the modern retail field.
It says postal/address infrastructure is one of the public layers that makes
modern terrain legible.

## How Future Modules Should Use A.1

A.1 should be the starting layer for later lineage modules:

- `A.2+market-town-to-mall` can compare market-center functions with retail
  complexes, malls, grocery, pharmacy, and mass retail.
- `A.3+trade-road-to-car-corridor` can compare route and exchange functions
  with QSR, auto parts, and gas/convenience sidecars.
- `A.4+port-district-to-logistics-retail` can compare movement infrastructure
  with port, event, industrial, and logistics-adjacent fields.
- `A.5+guild-quarter-to-professional-services` can compare local record and
  service functions with bank, insurance, tax-prep, and professional-service
  sidecars.

Each future module should preserve the A.1 discipline: historical analogy,
modern public infrastructure, observed commercial footprint, and model
hypothesis are separate evidence classes.
