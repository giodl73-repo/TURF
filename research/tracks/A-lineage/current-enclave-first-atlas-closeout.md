## Closeout Decision

The first atlas is useful as a claim-design tool. It shows that the central
spine can classify current-enclave terrain by packet role:

- full stack,
- highway-service stack,
- corridor-service partial movement,
- depot/intermodal plus service profile gap,
- ferry-side design-only contrast,
- light-control service-quarter contrast.

The next work should not be broad category expansion. It should either draft a
human-readable atlas summary from the six packets or choose one claim surface
and close only the evidence that would materially change it.

## Evidence Decisions

Long Beach / Lakewood needs no immediate source closeout for its current claim.

Arlington / Grand Prairie only needs new truck-stop evidence if the claim is
about truck-stop coverage rather than highway-service texture.

Cherry Hill / Moorestown only needs new motel/truck-stop acquisition if the
claim must compare lodging or complete truck-stop coverage.

Loop / River North only needs alternate loan/mortgage sourcing if lender-edge
texture is central to the claim.

Kingston WA should stay design-only unless A.4 is explicitly scored and broader
provisioning evidence is added. It should not be promoted by implication.

Kitsap Mall / Silverdale should stay a light-control packet unless direct
movement evidence or alternate LAN / loan-mortgage sources are needed.

## Claim Boundary

The atlas supports observable current-enclave stack classification only. It
does not establish official boundaries, lived identity, demographics, resident
identity, service adequacy, catchments, demand, traffic models, spending,
freight flow, truck counts, trip purpose, transaction volume, professional
quality, service absence, or individual behavior.
