# Washington Category Spread Comparison

## Scope

This report compares two reviewed Overture-derived Washington retail layers:

- Home improvement: Home Depot and Lowe's.
- Auto parts: Advance Auto Parts, AutoZone, NAPA Auto Parts, and O'Reilly Auto
  Parts.

It uses the checked-in packet-ready fixtures, Census 2020 ZCTA-county joins,
and Census July 2023 CBSA context. The report is public footprint evidence, not
an official corporate territory map.

## Statewide Brand Shape

Home improvement is a two-brand, winner-led spread:

| Brand | Stores | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Home Depot | 57 | 0.620 |
| Lowe's | 35 | 0.380 |

Auto parts is a four-brand, fragmented spread:

| Brand | Stores | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| NAPA Auto Parts | 190 | 0.375 |
| O'Reilly Auto Parts | 184 | 0.363 |
| AutoZone | 106 | 0.209 |
| Advance Auto Parts | 27 | 0.053 |

The difference is not just scale. Home improvement has one consistent statewide
leader. Auto parts has two near-peer statewide leaders, with AutoZone as a large
third network and Advance as a smaller Washington presence.

## Metro Shape

| Geography | Home improvement | Auto parts |
|---|---|---|
| Seattle-Tacoma-Bellevue CBSA | Home Depot leads 26 to 16. | O'Reilly is largest with 89 of 197, but the metro remains contested. |
| Bremerton-Silverdale-Port Orchard CBSA | Home Depot and Lowe's tie 2 to 2. | NAPA leads with 9 of 17. |

The Seattle home-improvement layer says "Home Depot-led big-box market." The
Seattle auto-parts layer says "O'Reilly largest, but dense and contested."

The Kitsap/Bremerton difference is stronger. Home improvement is balanced but
thin. Auto parts is locally dense and NAPA-led.

## Interpretation

Home improvement behaves like a destination big-box category. It has fewer
stores, fewer cities, fewer ZCTA candidates, and a stable Home Depot lead.
Market boundaries are larger and more regional.

Auto parts behaves like a neighborhood and corridor service category. It is
more than five times denser, reaches far more ZIP-derived ZCTA candidates, and
has no single statewide winner in the same sense. The important auto-parts
geography is the local mesh: mirrored competitors under a mile apart, service
coverage in small towns, and ferry-side places that home improvement misses.

For TURF, the lesson is that category type changes the map. A "city" can be a
big-box destination market in one category and a dense service grid in another.
